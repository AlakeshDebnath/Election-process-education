What & Why

India's Electoral System

India follows a First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) system within a Parliamentary democracy. Citizens elect representatives to the Lok Sabha (lower house) and State Assemblies every five years.

🏛️

Parliamentary Democracy

India is a sovereign democratic republic. The President is the constitutional head, but real executive power rests with the Prime Minister and the Cabinet.

🗳️

Two Types of Elections

General Elections (Lok Sabha) elect 543 MPs. State Assembly Elections (Vidhan Sabha) elect MLAs for each state legislature.

📍

Constituencies

India is divided into 543 Parliamentary constituencies. Each constituency elects one Member of Parliament (MP) using the FPTP method — the candidate with the most votes wins.

📱

Electronic Voting

India uses Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) with VVPAT (Voter Verified Paper Audit Trail) slips to ensure transparency and accuracy in vote counting.

⚖️

Universal Adult Franchise

Every Indian citizen aged 18 years and above has the right to vote, regardless of caste, religion, gender, or economic status.

🔒

Secret Ballot

Your vote is completely private. No one — not the government, party, or anyone else — can know whom you voted for. This protects voters from coercion.

Who Runs It?

Key Constitutional Bodies

Free and fair elections are protected by independent institutions with constitutional authority.

⚖️

Election Commission of India (ECI)

An autonomous constitutional body established in 1950. Responsible for administering elections to Parliament, state legislatures, and the offices of President and Vice-President.

  • Announces election schedule (Model Code of Conduct)
  • Registers political parties and manages symbols
  • Oversees EVMs and polling stations
  • Enforces the Model Code of Conduct
👨‍⚖️

Chief Election Commissioner

Heads the ECI. Appointed by the President of India. Can only be removed through a process similar to a Supreme Court judge — ensuring independence.

🏢

Returning Officers

Each constituency has a Returning Officer (RO), usually a senior IAS officer. Responsible for conducting the election in that constituency — accepting nominations, organizing polling, and declaring results.

👮

Central & State Police Forces

Security for polling stations is managed by central paramilitary forces (CRPF, BSF) and state police to prevent booth capturing and ensure peaceful polling.

📡

Media & Observer Corps

The ECI deploys General Observers (IAS) and Expenditure Observers (IRS) to monitor campaigning, spending, and polling day conduct in every constituency.

Your Vote Matters

The Voter's Journey

From registering to casting your ballot — here's everything you need to know as a first-time or returning voter.

How to Register as a Voter

1
Check Eligibility – You must be 18+ years old and an Indian citizen.
2
Visit voters.eci.gov.in or use the Voter Helpline App (available on iOS & Android).
3
Fill Form 6 – Provide your name, address, date of birth, and upload a photo.
4
Submit Documents – Age proof (Aadhaar, Birth Certificate) and address proof.
5
Receive EPIC – Your Elector's Photo Identity Card (Voter ID) will be issued.
💡 You can also register offline at your nearest Booth Level Officer (BLO) or Electoral Registration Officer (ERO).

Preparing for Polling Day

1
Find Your Polling Station – Use electoralsearch.eci.gov.in to locate your booth.
2
Know Your Candidates – Research candidates' affidavits on the ECI portal before voting.
3
Carry Valid ID – Voter ID (EPIC) or any 12 approved alternate IDs (Aadhaar, Passport, PAN, etc.).
4
Check Voter Slip – Your Booth Level Officer may deliver a Voter Information Slip with your serial number.
5
Note Polling Hours – Usually 7 AM to 6 PM. Go early to avoid queues.

Casting Your Vote

1
Join the Queue – Arrive at the polling station and wait in line. Separate queues for men and women.
2
Identity Verification – Show your EPIC/alternate ID to the polling officer. Your name is checked against the electoral roll.
3
Indelible Ink – Ink is applied to your left index finger to prevent double voting.
4
Enter Voting Compartment – You enter the private screened area with the EVM.
5
Press the Button – Press the blue button next to your chosen candidate on the EVM. A beep confirms your vote.
6
VVPAT Confirmation – A paper slip showing the candidate name and symbol is visible for 7 seconds before being cut and stored.

Vote Counting & Results

1
Counting Day – Usually 1–2 weeks after the last phase of polling. Announced by the ECI.
2
Counting Centres – Each constituency has a designated counting centre (usually a govt building) with strict security.
3
EVM Verification – EVMs are matched with their seals, then powered on and votes tallied round by round.
4
VVPAT Verification – 5 EVMs per assembly segment are randomly selected for VVPAT count to cross-verify electronic counts.
5
Winner Declared – The candidate with the highest votes in FPTP wins. Returning Officer issues the certificate of election.
📺 Results are broadcast live on news channels and also on the ECI's official Results website in real time.

Fair Play Rules

Model Code of Conduct

Once the election schedule is announced, the Model Code of Conduct (MCC) kicks in immediately — binding on all parties and candidates.

✅

Allowed

  • Peaceful public rallies with permission
  • Distributing party manifestos
  • Canvassing door-to-door
  • Advertising with prior approval
🚫

Banned

  • Announcing new govt schemes after MCC
  • Using government resources for campaigning
  • Bribing voters (money, gifts, liquor)
  • Hate speech / communal appeals
  • Campaigning within 48 hrs of polling (silent period)
